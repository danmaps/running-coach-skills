# Recovery Read

Read and apply the [shared coaching rules](../../references/coaching-contract.md) before proceeding.

Watch the user's recovery signals the way a good coach watches an athlete's face: quietly, in context, and only speaking up when a pattern is real.

## Signals

Use whatever the user's data sources provide: sleep duration and quality, resting heart rate, heart rate variability if available, plus the user's reported feel from debriefs and check-ins. Retrieve signals only from accessible, permitted sources under the shared data rules; do not assume that a health-store connection exposes them. Signals the user does not track are simply absent; never fill them in.

## Process

1. Read the profile, accepted plan, and observations. Establish the user's baseline from at least a few weeks of comparable personal data. Note device/source changes and gaps; if no adequate baseline exists, say so and avoid declaring a trend. All judgments are against the user's normal, never population averages presented as verdicts.
2. Compare the recent stretch (about 7 days) to that baseline, alongside the training load from the same period.
3. Reconcile findings with workout-sizer or weekly-check-in into one recommendation. Save evidence and factual observations without an extra approval step; obtain acceptance for future prescription changes. Patterns worth naming:
   - Resting heart rate elevated several days running while sleep is short and training load rose: recommend an easier week or extra rest day, and write the change into the plan if accepted.
   - Sleep collapsing while the plan is in a build phase: flag the mismatch; the plan may need to fit the life this month, not the ideal.
   - Everything normal: say nothing, or confirm in one line during a check-in. A recovery skill that cries wolf gets ignored.
4. Distinguish a single bad night (noise) from a trend (signal). Require trends before changing a prescription for ordinary wearable variation. This requirement never delays responding to reported pain, illness, unusual symptoms, or an immediate safety concern; normal readings do not override those reports.

## Guardrails

- Consumer health data is noisy. Present readings as trends with uncertainty, never as precise measurements of readiness.
- This skill gives training guidance only. It never interprets health data medically. Symptoms, illness, or anything that worries the user goes to a clinician, and training pauses in the meantime.
- Never shame rest. Rest taken on data is training working as intended.
